# S1: OpenF1 data reality check

Spike S1 ([08 §3](../08-implementation-plan.md#s1--data-reality-check--1-day-after-m1)) asks what real OpenF1 data looks like for the cases the algorithms must handle, before M2 builds them. Tracked in [#14](https://github.com/jbird/race-replay/issues/14).

> **Status:** done. The analysis pipeline (centreline, projection, progress, gaps, validators) arrives in M2 and M3, so NFR-05 (Q6) is answered here by a proxy on OpenF1's own data. RR-080 ([#42](https://github.com/jbird/race-replay/issues/42)) confirms it with the real pipeline in M7.

## Setup

- **Script:** [`scripts/s1-explore.cs`](../../scripts/s1-explore.cs), a .NET 10 file-based app with a minimal client. It caches raw responses under `.cache/openf1/s1/` and writes CSVs to `reports/s1/`; both folders are gitignored.

  ```bash
  dotnet run scripts/s1-explore.cs -- analyse 9662 9165 9636
  ```

  The first run made 86 requests. A second run made none and printed identical numbers.
- **Races:**

  | Race | Session key | Why | Laps | Notes |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2024 Abu Dhabi GP, Yas Marina | 9662 | Permanent circuit | 58 | Dry; lap-1 incident, VSC, lapped cars |
  | 2023 Singapore GP, Marina Bay | 9165 | Street circuit | 62 | SC, VSC, pit stops under the SC |
  | 2024 São Paulo GP, Interlagos | 9636 | Red flag | 69 | Wet; VSC, two SCs, red flag, rolling restart; two non-starters and a pit-lane start |

  The red-flag race was found by querying `race_control?flag=RED` for every 2023–2025 race. Eight had one: 2023 Melbourne, Zandvoort, Mexico City and Interlagos; 2024 Suzuka, Monaco and Interlagos; and 2025 Spa. These three races are the M7 fixture races for RR-080.
- **Numbers** cover the racing window: lap 1 start to the chequered flag, or a retired car's last lap. "Per car-hour" divides by the racing time summed over cars: 26.8, 31.9 and 39.8 car-hours.

## Answers

### Coordinate units

The path length of clean laps compares with the official lap length as follows. A clean lap is lap 3 or later, not a pit lap, and within 103 % of the median lap time.

| Race | Clean laps | Units per metre: median (p5–p95) |
|---|---|---|
| Abu Dhabi (5,281 m) | 903 | 9.872 (9.832–9.905) |
| Singapore (4,940 m) | 921 | 9.806 (9.752–9.844) |
| São Paulo (4,309 m) | 822 | 9.764 (9.700–9.816) |

**Units are decimetres: `UnitsPerMetre = 10` is confirmed.** The measured laps come out 1.3–2.4 % short because they follow the racing line, which cuts corners, and the sampling chords cut them a little more. The official length runs along the centreline. A centreline built from these traces will therefore measure about 1–3 % under the official length, inside `CentrelineLengthValidator`'s ±5 %.

### Location quality

| Measure | Abu Dhabi | Singapore | São Paulo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sampling Δt: mean / p50 / p95 / p99.9 | 0.259 / 0.240 / 0.420 / 1.04 s | 0.263 / 0.240 / 0.420 / 1.10 s | 0.258 / 0.240 / 0.420 / 1.04 s |
| Rate | 3.87 Hz | 3.80 Hz | 3.88 Hz |
| Gaps over 2 s while running | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `(0,0,0)` between a car's first and last real position | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `(0,0,0)` in total (whole session) | 63,694 | 43,122 | 133,538 |
| Frozen positions (same x, y ≥ 1 s while moving) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Duplicate or out-of-order timestamps | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Consecutive pairs implying > 120 m/s | 9,361 (4,188 with Δt < 0.2 s) | 4,746 | 5,887 |
| Pairs ≥ 0.5 s apart implying > 120 m/s, per car-hour | 1.91 | 2.38 | 0.13 |
| Speed p99.9 / max over pairs ≥ 0.5 s apart | 112.7 / 184.1 m/s | 108.2 / 219.9 m/s | 107.3 / 123.6 m/s |

- **The feed is regular.** Every driver in a session has the same number of samples (34,408–34,421, 39,931 and 46,559), so OpenF1 resamples onto a common clock. Running cars have no gaps over 2 s.
- **`(0,0,0)` only marks a car that isn't running:** before it first moves, after it stops or retires, and for non-starters. São Paulo car 23 is zeros all session, and car 18 is zeros from its formation-lap stop onwards. None appear mid-run.
- **Timestamps jitter, positions don't.** Speeds from consecutive pairs exceed 120 m/s 150–350 times per car-hour, about half of them with Δt < 0.2 s. Measured over ≥ 0.5 s, real glitches are 0.1–2.4 per car-hour. The cleaner's spike rule in 03 §4 needs both neighbours to be implausible, so it already tolerates jitter. Any single-pair speed test would not.
- **The "projection snaps" question needs the projector (M2).** S1 counted implausible jumps instead (the rows above).

### Race start (Q2)

| Race | Scheduled | Lap 1 `date_start` | Motion onset from the grid (median) | Race control at that second |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abu Dhabi | 13:00:00 | 13:03:35.033, the same for all 20 | +0.7 s | `SessionStatus`: `SESSION STARTED`; `Flag` `GREEN`: `GREEN LIGHT - PIT EXIT OPEN` |
| Singapore | 12:00:00 | 12:03:44.658, the same for 18 starters | +1.0 s | the same two messages |
| São Paulo | 15:30:00 (delayed) | 15:49:57.515, the same for 17 of 18 | +1.0 s | the same two messages |

- **Lap 1 `date_start` is the race start (lights out)**, the same timestamp for every starter, and it matches race control's `SESSION STARTED`. The scheduled time is the formation lap.
- **Exceptions:**
  - **Pit-lane starters have no lap 1 record.** Their laps begin at lap 2: Singapore car 24 (62 laps) and São Paulo car 55 (39 laps).
  - **Non-starters:** São Paulo car 23 has no laps. Car 18 stopped on the formation lap and is classified DNS, yet has a single lap 1 record at the restart, 17:02:00, 72 minutes after the start.
- **Recommendation:**
  - **Signal:** *T₁* = the first `SESSION STARTED` after the scheduled start, which is the same instant as the most common lap 1 `date_start`.
  - **Fallback:** the motion onset median minus 1 s.
  - **Don't** take each car's own lap 1 as its start (03 §6 lap anchoring); use the race's *T₁*. A pit-lane starter has none, and a non-starter's can be wrong. Treat a car classified DNS as not running.
- `GREEN LIGHT - PIT EXIT OPEN` alone is not enough: it also appears when the pit exit opens 40–60 minutes before the race.

### `pit.date` (Q1)

70 stops matched against each car's speed profile. The stop is the stationary stretch nearest `date`. Entry and exit are where the car enters and leaves the pit-lane limiter band.

| Race | Stops | `date` − entry | `date` − stop start | `date` − exit | (`date` − `lane_duration`) − entry |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abu Dhabi | 28 | 21.2 s | 12.7 s | **−0.2 s** | −1.2 s |
| Singapore | 25 | 24.3 s | 13.0 s | **−0.3 s** | −7.8 s |
| São Paulo (wet) | 17 | 23.0 s | 14.3 s | **−3.3 s** | −2.9 s |

- **`pit.date` marks the pit-lane exit.** It is within 0.3 s of it at both dry races, and about 3 s early in the wet one.
- **The pit window** is therefore [`date − lane_duration`, `date`]. `lane_duration` runs a few seconds longer than our limiter band, because the timing loops sit outside the speed-limit lines.
- **`stop_duration`** matches the measured stationary time to −0.2 s (median). It is absent before the 2024 US GP, as 04 §2 says.
- **During a red flag, cars waiting in the pit lane get `pit` records:** 18 at São Paulo. `PitStopExtractor` must drop records inside a `Suspended` segment.

### `race_control` wording

Exact strings seen, as `category` / `flag` / `message`:

| Event | Records |
|---|---|
| Race start | `SessionStatus` / — / `SESSION STARTED`, with `Flag` / `GREEN` / `GREEN LIGHT - PIT EXIT OPEN` (scope `Track`) |
| Safety car | `SafetyCar` / — / `SAFETY CAR DEPLOYED` … `SAFETY CAR IN THIS LAP` (no "ENDING" message for the SC) |
| Virtual safety car | `SafetyCar` / — / `VIRTUAL SAFETY CAR DEPLOYED` … `VIRTUAL SAFETY CAR ENDING` |
| Red flag | `SessionStatus` / — / `SESSION ABORTED`, then `Flag` / `RED` / `RED FLAG` (scope `Track`), a second apart |
| Resumption | `Other` / — / `RACE WILL RESUME AT 14:02` (local time), `ORDER FOR RESTART: 31, 1, 10, …`, `RACE WILL RESUME AT 14:06 - ROLLING START PROCEDURE`, then `SessionStatus` / — / `SESSION STARTED` at the restart |
| Finish | `Flag` / `CHEQUERED` / `CHEQUERED FLAG` and `SessionStatus` / — / `SESSION FINISHED`, within 4 s of each other in either order |

`SessionStatus` makes the clearest segment boundaries:
- `Racing` starts at `SESSION STARTED`;
- `Suspended` runs from `SESSION ABORTED` to the next `SESSION STARTED`;
- the finish is at `CHEQUERED FLAG`.

"Resume at" times are local and get revised, so they are for display only.

### `intervals` and `position`

| Measure | Abu Dhabi | Singapore | São Paulo |
|---|---|---|---|
| `intervals` records | 23,288 | 24,439 | 21,060 |
| Value forms (both fields) | number 43,293; `"+N LAP"` 2,817; `null` 466 | number 48,373; `null` 505 | number 41,710; `null` 344; `"+N LAP"` 63; `"+N LAPS"` 3 |
| Cadence per driver: p50 / p95 | 3.46 / 7.43 s | 4.22 / 7.36 s | 3.86 / 8.10 s |
| `position` records (before lap 1) | 508 (20) | 405 (19) | 360 (20) |
| Time between `position` records: p50 / p95 | 0.0 / 80.1 s | 0.0 / 79.4 s | 0.0 / 111.6 s |

- **Only the forms the `GapValue` converter expects appear:** a number, `"+N LAP"`, `"+N LAPS"` and `null`. There was no other string.
- **`position` comes in bursts.** Changes arrive together (p50 0 s apart), then nothing for a minute or more. Each driver has one record before lap 1 (the grid).
- **`intervals` has long silences:** the longest gap per driver is 58–72 minutes, against a p95 of 7–8 s. S1 didn't trace where those gaps fall; the validators only compare samples within 3 s.

### Response size and time

- **Whole-session `location` per driver:** 4.3–6.0 MB, 34,000–47,000 samples, fetched in 1.9–5.2 s (median 2.4–3.2 s). No request timed out, so adaptive windowing (04 §4) is a fallback these races never needed. The 60 s attempt timeout (04 §3.1) has plenty of headroom.
- **A whole race is 87–119 MB of `location` alone**, more than 04 §4's estimate of 70–80 MB in total. 04 §4 is updated.
- **Rate limiting:** the red-flag search (70 requests, paced at 3 per second and 30 per minute) drew two `429`s, with `Retry-After` of 1 s and 60 s. The analysis runs drew none. The production client (RR-041, [#23](https://github.com/jbird/race-replay/issues/23)) must honour `Retry-After`.

### NFR-05, first read (Q6)

The validators need derived gaps (M2), so S1 compares OpenF1's two timing sources instead.

- **Gaps:** a lap-line gap from `laps.date_start` (car minus leader, same lap) against the nearest `intervals.gap_to_leader` within 3 s. Green-flag laps from lap 3, with pit in and out laps and SC, VSC and red-flag periods excluded (03 §11).
- **Order:** the rank at the lap line (who crossed the start of lap *n* first) against the `position` step function, with ±5 s tolerance.

| Race | Gap pairs | \|difference\| median | p95 | Within 0.5 s | Order agreement at the line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abu Dhabi | 705 | 0.056 s | 0.152 s | 100.0 % | 100.0 % (892) |
| Singapore | 830 | 0.028 s | 0.190 s | 99.9 % | 100.0 % (911) |
| São Paulo | 766 | 0.053 s | 0.162 s | 99.9 % | 100.0 % (816) |

- **OpenF1's references are sharp:** its two sources agree to about 0.05 s. Measurement noise in the reference won't stop our derived gaps meeting **0.5 s median and 1.5 s p95**, so the thresholds are realistic. Whether our own progress model meets them is RR-080's question.
- **Running order:** `position` is consistent with the lap-line order. The 95 % threshold stays, because our derived order is continuous between lines, where the official one only changes at timing loops.
- **NFR-05 stays provisional until RR-080** confirms it with the real pipeline.

## Recorded fixtures

Trimmed excerpts for the adapter tests (06 §9) are under [`tests/RaceReplay.TestKit/Fixtures/OpenF1/`](../../tests/RaceReplay.TestKit/Fixtures/OpenF1/). Each holds 2 drivers and 75–90 s, cut by the script; 273 KB of JSON in all, against a 300 KB cap. Each folder's README carries the CC BY-NC-SA notice and what it covers:

- `2024-abu-dhabi`: a pit stop by a lapped car, numeric and `"+1 LAP"` intervals, the leader.
- `2023-singapore`: `SAFETY CAR DEPLOYED`, and two pit stops under it.
- `2024-sao-paulo`: `SESSION ABORTED` and `RED FLAG`, a car stopped after its crash, and a non-starter whose positions are all `(0,0,0)`.

06 §9 suggested about 5 minutes per excerpt. That would be about 240 KB of `location` each, so the windows are shorter. `RecordedFixtureTests` guards the cap, the endpoints, the two-driver limit and the licence notice.
